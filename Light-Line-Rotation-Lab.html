Interactive companion to M. Madhi, “Special relativity: a geometric representation by rotation about the light line” (version 3)

Light-Line Rotation Lab

The article does relativistic kinematics with one hand-drawn triangle and one rule. Every plate below runs that rule live, checks it against the full Lorentz matrices, and ends with what the check found.

The rule

V + A∥→k (V + A∥) V − A∥→(V − A∥) / k A⊥→A⊥ (unchanged)

k = √((1 + β)/(1 − β)) is the Doppler factor. This carries a state from the moving frame back to the lab; the reverse direction swaps k and 1/k. The new triangle is read back as V = (sum + difference)/2 and A∥ = (sum − difference)/2.

At D = 1/4 the triangle gives V = 5/4, A = 3/4, V + A = 2 = kC and V − A = 1/2 = C/k, as in Figure 2. The split A² = D² + 2CD is an exact identity, and √(2CD) is the Newtonian momentum √(2mD) times c. The two parts are algebraic pieces, not directions in space.

§4 · App. B · Fig. 3

Rotation about the light line

Rotate the x and t axes about the light line x = t by θ, with cos θ = (1 − β)/(1 + β). Their shadow on the (x, t) plane points along the moving frame's axes. Multiplying by k carries each shadow out to the hyperbola.

Checked

The construction lands exactly on the Lorentz axes: the shadow's slope is tan²(θ/2) = β, and k times the shadow is (γ, γβ), on the hyperbola. As §4 of the paper states, the inputs are those of Bondi's k-calculus: θ is chosen so that the slope is β, the scale comes from (V + A)(V − A) = C², and energy–momentum transforms like (t, x). The drawing reconstructs the transformation and makes it visible.

§5 · §6

Stacking boosts on one line

Boosts along one line multiply their Doppler factors, so ln k adds. With K = kn the speed is (K² − 1)/(K² + 1). It approaches c but never reaches it, because (V + A)(V − A) = C² survives every boost.

    §5 · App. D

    Thomas precession

    A body on a circular orbit receives N small boosts per revolution, each toward where its velocity has turned. The small Wigner rotations add up, so a gyroscope carried by the body falls behind the lab axes by 2π(γ − 1) = 2πD/C per revolution.

    0.50
    120
    Checked

    Composing small boosts reproduces 2π(γ − 1) per revolution, and the match tightens as N grows. For hydrogen, 13.606 eV and 510.999 keV give 1.673 × 10−4 rad per revolution. The Earth's 0.64″ per century is one part of its geodetic precession: the Earth is in free fall, so its Thomas precession is not measured separately.

    §5

    Aberration of starlight

    Light is the triangle with C = 0. Starlight arriving perpendicular to the Earth's motion has A⊥ = V and A∥ = 0. The rule gives A∥ = γβV and leaves A⊥ alone, so the light tilts by sin a = β.

    Checked

    20.49″ at 29.78 km/s. The IAU (1976) constant of aberration, 20.4955″, is a derived value equal to v/c at the Earth's mean orbital speed of 29.789 km/s. Press “29.789 · IAU basis” and the rule gives 20.4955″ as well.

    §5 · ref. 12

    Muon lifetime in the CERN storage ring

    Time dilation is the triangle's ratio V/C = γ. Bailey et al. (1977) stored muons at γ = 29.33 and timed their decays in flight.

    Checked

    29.33 × 2.197 = 64.44 µs. The μ+ measurement, 64.419 ± 0.058 µs, agrees within a fraction of its error. The μ− value, 64.368 ± 0.029 µs, is shown for completeness but is not an independent test: Bailey et al. assumed special relativity to extract the μ− rest lifetime from it.

    The imaginary branch

    When V is smaller than C, A² = V² − C² is negative and A is imaginary. A waveguide shows both branches: its dispersion relation f² = fc² + (ck/2π)² is the same triangle, with the cutoff frequency as C.
